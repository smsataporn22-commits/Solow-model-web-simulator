What does Y = A·√K mean?

Output (Y) depends on the capital stock (K) and the technology level (A). The square root means diminishing returns to capital: each extra unit of capital adds less additional output than the one before. A higher A shifts the whole production curve upward.

Y = A·√K I = s·Y D = δ·K ΔK = s·Y − δ·K K* = (s·A/δ)² Y* = A·√K*,  C* = (1−s)·Y*
  • Saving rate (s) ↑ – more output is invested, the investment curve rotates up, and the steady-state capital K* rises.
  • Depreciation rate (δ) ↑ – capital wears out faster, the depreciation line gets steeper, and K* falls.
  • Technology (A) ↑ – each unit of capital produces more, so both Y and I shift upward and K*, Y* rise (since Y* = s·A²/δ, output grows with the square of A).
  • Capital grows while I > D (ΔK > 0), shrinks while I < D, and stops at the steady state where I = D.
